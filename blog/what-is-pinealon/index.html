What Is Pinealon? Neuronal Peptide (Glu-Asp-Arg) Research Explained

Research Overview · September 2026

Pinealon is a short-peptide bioregulator studied mainly in neuroscience research, where it is examined for its interactions with neuronal cell models and its role in oxidative-stress pathways. This overview explains what Pinealon is, how researchers describe its mechanism, and what it is studied for, all within a strictly research-focused frame.

Pinealon is also written as EDR, after the single-letter code for its three amino acids. Both names refer to the same molecule, so you will see them used interchangeably in the literature.

What It Is

Pinealon is a synthetic tripeptide made of the amino acids glutamate, aspartate, and arginine (Glu-Asp-Arg). It belongs to the class of short-peptide bioregulators originally derived from pineal-gland research, the same family that includes Epithalon.

Its main point of interest for researchers is its studied role in the central nervous system: how it interacts with neuronal cell models and how it relates to reactive oxygen species and gene-expression pathways under oxidative stress.

What Researchers Study It For

Neuronal cell survival and oxidative-stress research
Peptide bioregulator gene-expression mechanisms
Cognitive and memory research paradigms (animal models)
Circadian and pineal-pathway investigations

These are the areas that appear most often in the Pinealon literature. Each is an investigational research direction, a question being studied, not a demonstrated benefit or an approved use.

Common Research Protocols

Pinealon is supplied as a lyophilized powder and reconstituted with bacteriostatic water. In preclinical research it is typically administered by injection and studied for effects on neuronal oxidative-stress markers and behavioral endpoints in animal models.

In cell-model research, Pinealon has been reported to reduce the accumulation of reactive oxygen species and to support cell viability under oxidative-stress conditions. Source

Community Interest Areas

Pinealon is studied within the broader class of Khavinson short-peptide bioregulators and draws interest in neuroscience research for its examined interactions with neuronal cell models. It is often investigated alongside other pineal-derived peptides such as Epithalon.

Pinealon vs. Epithalon

Pinealon and Epithalon are both pineal-derived short-peptide bioregulators, and they are frequently mentioned together. They are, however, distinct compounds studied for different pathways.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Pinealon?

Pinealon (also called EDR) is a synthetic tripeptide made of three amino acids, Glu-Asp-Arg. It belongs to the class of short-peptide bioregulators first characterized in pineal-gland research. What is Pinealon made of?

Pinealon is a tripeptide composed of glutamic acid, aspartic acid, and arginine (Glu-Asp-Arg). It is often referred to by the single-letter sequence EDR. It is a synthetic short-peptide bioregulator, not an extract.

What do researchers study Pinealon for?

Reported research areas include neuronal cell survival and oxidative-stress research, peptide bioregulator gene-expression mechanisms, cognitive and memory research paradigms in animal models, and circadian and pineal-pathway investigations. How is Pinealon different from Epithalon?

Both are pineal-derived short-peptide bioregulators, but they are distinct compounds. Pinealon is a tripeptide (Glu-Asp-Arg) studied mainly for neuronal cell models and oxidative stress in the central nervous system. Epithalon is a tetrapeptide (Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly) studied mainly for telomerase and telomere length.
