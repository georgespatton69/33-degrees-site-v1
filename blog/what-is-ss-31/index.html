What Is SS-31? Elamipretide & Mitochondrial Membrane Research

Research Overview · September 2026

SS-31 is studied in mitochondrial research for a distinctive reason: rather than acting as a general antioxidant, it is examined for a targeted action at the inner mitochondrial membrane, the site where cellular energy is produced. This overview explains what SS-31 is, how researchers describe its mechanism, and what it is studied for, all within a strictly research-focused frame.

SS-31 is also known as elamipretide (and by the earlier research code MTP-131). All three names refer to the same molecule, so you will see them used interchangeably in the literature.

What It Is

SS-31 is a small, cell-permeable peptide that selectively targets the inner mitochondrial membrane. It binds to cardiolipin, a lipid molecule critical for maintaining the structure and function of the electron transport chain where ATP is produced. Source

This targeted mechanism is what sets SS-31 apart in mitochondrial research: it concentrates at the exact location where energy production takes place, rather than acting broadly across the cell.

What Researchers Study It For

Mitochondrial membrane integrity
Reactive oxygen species reduction
Age-related cardiac and renal function
Electron transport chain efficiency

These are the areas that appear most often in the SS-31 literature. Each is an investigational research direction, a question being studied, not a demonstrated benefit or an approved use.

Common Research Protocols

SS-31 is typically reconstituted from lyophilized powder and administered via subcutaneous injection. Its small size and positive charge allow it to concentrate selectively in mitochondria, which is central to how researchers study its targeted mechanism.

Community Interest Areas

SS-31 has drawn research interest for its highly targeted mechanism, acting directly at the site where mitochondrial energy production occurs. Investigators are particularly interested in its examined ability to restore function in aged mitochondria rather than simply providing antioxidant support.

SS-31 vs. MOTS-c

SS-31 and MOTS-c are both studied within mitochondrial research, and they are often compared. They act, however, on very different targets, one at the membrane and one through signaling.
